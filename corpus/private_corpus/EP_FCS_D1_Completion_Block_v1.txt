Chapter 1
Purpose and Position
This document should be read as the D1 completion layer rather than as a free-standing
alternative theorem note. The earlier D1 block established four initial families: closure, weak
consistency, persistence-under-maintenance, and fragility monotonicity. The D1 extension block
then added partial independence, structural reproduction, quantitative persistence bounds, and
fracture-threshold ideas in preliminary form. The present completion block consolidates the
missing Tier I families in a tighter and more explicit mother-body proof format.
The strategic function of this document is simple. Tier I is the layer that secures identity before
expansion. Until Tier I is substantially complete, later operator, projection, reconstruction,
validity, compression, and algorithmic results remain under-protected. The present block
therefore aims to close the most important remaining Tier I gaps before D2 begins.

2.2 Core Typing Convention
A relational statement belongs to the admissible grammar only if its arguments satisfy the
required type signature. For example,
maintained_by(S,M ), depends_on(S,E ),
reproduces_through(S,R ), fractures_when(S,F )
are admissible only when the arguments belong to the required typed domains.
Definition 2.1(Well-Formed Relational Expression). A relational expressionρ(o1,...,o k) is
well-formed if and only if:
1. ρbelongs to the admissible predicate familyR,
2. each argumentoi belongs to the declared domain of the corresponding slot ofρ,
3. the expression does not violate any dependency or exclusion rule already imposed by the
mother-body.
We writeWellFormed(ρ(o1,...,o k)) = 1 when these conditions hold.
Definition 2.2(Admissible Core Model). A candidate core modelM is admissible if it satisfies
the typing discipline, the relational dependency rules, and the core axiom set. We write
Admissible(M) = 1.

Relational Well-Formedness
3.1 Why This Theorem Family Matters
Closure at the ontological level is not enough. A model can use the right object names and still
generate malformed relations or illegal compositions. The point of this theorem family is to show
that, once the mother-body typing and dependency rules are enforced, admissible relational
chains stay inside the grammar rather than leaking into pseudo-EP syntax.
3.2 Main Theorem
Theorem 3.1(Relational Well-Formedness Under Typed Composition). LetM be an admissible
core model. Let
ρ1(o1,...,o k), ρ 2(o′
1,...,o′
m)
be well-formed relational expressions inM such that the output type ofρ1 matches the required
input type of the composition rule joiningρ1 to ρ2. If the mother-body dependency and exclusion
rules are respected, then the composed chain
ρ2◦ρ1
is also well-formed.
Proof. BecauseM is admissible, every participating relation is typed and every argument
occupies a permitted slot. By hypothesis, the output type ofρ1 matches the input requirement
for the composition rule, so the composition is not blocked by type mismatch. Since dependency
and exclusion rules are also assumed satisfied, no illegal transition is introduced at the composition
boundary. Therefore the resulting chain remains inside the admissible relational grammar. Hence
WellFormed(ρ2◦ρ1) = 1.
Corollary 3.2 (Well-Formedness of Canonical Chains). In any admissible core model, the
canonical chains

Chapter 4
Partial Independence of the Core Ax-
ioms
4.1 Why Independence Matters
A weakly consistent axiom set is useful, but it can still be trivial if the axioms collapse into
disguised restatements. The correct Tier I goal is not absolute logical independence of every
sentence under every future formalization. The more realistic and useful target is partial
independence: at least some axioms contribute non-redundant structural information to the
mother-body.
4.2 Independence Framework
Let the core axiom family be denoted
A ={A1,A 2,...,A n}.
For a given axiomAi, writeA−i =A\{Ai}.
Definition 4.1(Partial Independence). An axiomAi is independent relative to a theorem target
Θ if A⊢Θ but A−i ⊬ Θ.
This notion is enough for Tier I because it shows that removing a given axiom loses at least one
structurally important consequence.
4.3 Main Independence Result
Theorem 4.2(Partial Independence of Maintenance and Fragility Axioms). Under the cur-
rent mother-body formalization, the persistence-under-maintenance axiom and the suppressed-
correction-implies-fragility-accumulation axiom are partially independent in the following sense:
1. If the maintenance axiom is removed, persistence-under-maintenance can no longer be
derived.
2. If the fragility-accumulation axiom is removed, monotone growth of fragility under blocked
correction can no longer be derived.

Chapter 5
Structural Reproduction Theorem
5.1 The Target Claim
The structural reproduction axiom says that systems reproduce deep maintenance-control-
reproduction logic more fundamentally than they reproduce surface description. To complete
Tier I, this should not remain only an axiom statement. It should yield an explicit theorem
under a clear transmission condition.
5.2 Transmission Condition
Assumption 5.1(Operational Reproduction Transmission). Suppose a reproducing systemS
transmits enough of its maintenance, control, and reproduction organization to a successorS′
such that:
1. the maintenance channel remains viable,
2. the control logic remains executable under the successor constraint field,
3. the reproduction mechanism itself is carried into the successor,
4. surface narrative elements may vary without destroying those three structural transfers.
Theorem 5.2(Reproduction Preserves Structural Logic More Reliably Than Surface Form).
Under the operational reproduction transmission assumption, ifS reproduces throughR, then
there exists a successorS′such that the successor preserves the maintenance-control-reproduction
pattern ofS even when the surface form changes. Symbolically,
reproduces_through(S,R )⇒preserves(RMCR(S′))⪰preserves(RMCR(S))
with no requirement that
preserves(SurfaceForm(S′)) = preserves(SurfaceForm(S)).
In particular, structural reproduction is more robust than exact surface reproduction.
Proof. By assumption, reproduction transfers the viable maintenance channel, executable control
logic, and reproduction mechanism itself. These three together constitute the maintenance-
control-reproduction patternRMCR. Since surface narrative elements are allowed to vary without

Chapter 6
Fracture Threshold and Fragility-to-
Fracture Bridge
6.1 Why This Theorem Family Matters
The earlier D1 theorem block already secured fragility monotonicity under blocked correction.
The missing completion step is to connect that monotonic quantity to an actual regime transition.
Without that bridge, fragility remains an accumulating warning variable but not yet a theorem-
bearing predictor of fracture.
6.2 Threshold Setting
Let Φ crit(S,T t) denote the critical fragility threshold permitted by the system’s current mainte-
nance and stability-horizon structure.
Assumption 6.1(Threshold Regularity). Assume that for the system under study:
1. Φ crit(S,T t) is finite and well-defined over the horizon of analysis,
2. Φ crit weakly decreases as effective stability horizon collapses,
3. once Φ frag(t) exceeds Φ crit(S,T t), no admissible local correction inside the current step can
fully reabsorb the overshoot.
Theorem 6.2(Fracture Threshold Theorem). Assume threshold regularity. If
Φ frag(t)> Φ crit(S,T t),
then the system enters a fracture regime at timet; equivalently,
Φ frag(t)> Φ crit(S,T t)⇒fractures_when(S,F ).
Proof. By threshold regularity, the critical bound is finite and meaningful. Once actual fragility
exceeds that bound, the current maintenance-control organization can no longer stabilize the
accumulated contradiction within the available effective horizon. The third regularity clause
rules out instant internal absorption of the overshoot inside the same step. Therefore the system
cannot remain in the non-fracture regime after threshold crossing. Hence it enters a fracture
regime.

Corollary 6.3(Blocked Correction Plus Persistence Pressure Implies Eventual Fracture Under
Finite Threshold). Suppose:
1. correction remains blocked for a sustained interval,
2. fragility grows monotonically over that interval,
3. the critical fracture threshold remains finite.
Then if the interval is long enough, the system eventually fractures.
Proof. Monotone growth ofΦ frag(t) under blocked correction implies that fragility continues
to rise. If the critical threshold remains finite, then sufficiently prolonged monotone increase
eventually forcesΦ frag(t) above that threshold. The fracture threshold theorem then applies.
Proposition 6.4(Lower Stability Horizon Raises Fracture Exposure). If all else is fixed and the
effective stability horizonTt declines in a way that lowers or preservesΦ crit(S,T t), then fracture
exposure weakly increases.
Proof. A lower or equal threshold requires less accumulated fragility to trigger fracture. Therefore
the set of fragility states lying in the fracture regime weakly expands.

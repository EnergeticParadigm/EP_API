0. Identity Clause
0.1 Ontological Priority
Principle 0.1(Mother-Body Priority). Energetic Paradigm cannot take papers as its
ontology. Papers must take a reconstructable formal blueprint as their ontology.
The first identity claim of EP-FCS v0 is therefore not bibliographic but architectural.
The paradigm is defined primarily by a formal mother-body that can generate analyses,
constrain drift, and support partial reconstruction under loss. A paper may illuminate
the system, prove a fragment, instantiate a case, or stress-test a boundary, but no paper
is itself the mother-body.
Definition 0.1(EPRA). The total reconstructive architecture is defined as
EPRA =⟨Ω, R, A, ∆, O, Π, K, V⟩, (1)
where Ω is the ontology module,R the relational module,A the axiom module,∆ the
dynamics module,O the operator module,Π the projection module,K the reconstruction
module, andV the validation module.
These eight modules are not optional accessories. Together they form the minimum
complete mother-body required for a reconstructable Energetic Paradigm.
0.2 Three Non-Negotiable Requirements
The mother-body must satisfy three requirements simultaneously:
Generativity: EPRA ⇝ indefinitely many admissible analyses, (2)
Constraint: EPRA̸⇝ arbitrary pseudo-EP outputs, (3)
Reconstructability: partial loss does not imply total loss of structure. (4)
If any one of these fails, the resulting system is inadequate. A framework that generates
outputs but cannot exclude drift is too loose. A framework that constrains outputs but
cannot generate analyses is sterile. A framework that can generate and constrain but
cannot be reconstructed is too fragile to serve as the ontology of the paradigm.
0.3 Projection Status of Papers
Definition 0.2(Projection Paper). A paperPi is a structured projection of the mother-
body if and only if there exists a projection operatorπi such that
Pi =πi(EPRA). (5)

Under this clause, papers are not the source of the paradigm. They are visible slices of it.
Axiomatic papers, dynamics papers, case papers, failure-mode papers, and reconstruction
papers are all projections with different informational roles, not rival ontologies.
0.4 Reconstruction Requirement
Definition 0.3(Core Invariant Set). Let the core invariant set be
Icore ={Ω,R,A, ∆,O,V}. (6)
The mother-body is considered preserved if these invariants remain recoverable up to
structural equivalence.
Definition 0.4(Reconstruction Condition). LetQ be any surviving subset of projections
and fragments. The architecture satisfies reconstructability at thresholdτif
Coverage(Q)≥τ⇒ K(Q)∼=Icore EPRA. (7)
This means that the goal of reconstruction is not verbatim textual recovery. It is
structural recovery of the core formal body.
0.5 Identity Consequence
The direct consequence is methodological: work must begin with the mother-body itself.
The first executable document is therefore not “Paper 1” but the formal core specification.
Only after the core exists in typed, symbolic, and operational form do projection papers
become admissible.

1.1 Two-Layer Ontology
EP-FCS v0 adopts a two-layer ontology. The first layer defines system-level structural
objects. The second layer defines energetic primitives that make transfer, obstruction,
retention, and routing mathematically legible.
Definition 1.1(System-Level Object Set). Let the high-level object set be
Σ ={S,E,M,C,R,X,T,F,L,I,D }, (8)
where:

1.3 Bridge Between the Two Layers
The two ontological layers are not competitors. They are coupled. The high-level objects
specify what kinds of structural realities must be representable; the energetic primitives
specify how those realities become operationally traceable.
A schematic bridge can be written as:
E↦→s,
M↦→{p,m,c},
C↦→{b,p,I -conditioned routing},
X↦→{ℓshifted across targets},
F↦→instability or breakdown of viable path structure.
This bridge does not reduce the high-level system layer to a single low-level metaphor.
Rather, it provides an admissible translation interface between structural concepts and
energetic operations.

1.6 Ontological Priority Rule
Principle 1.1(Priority of Structure Over Surface). In EP-FCS v0, structural organization

This rule does not deny the relevance of discourse. It subordinates discourse to
structure.

relations may obtain among them. Without a typed relational grammar, the ontology
remains a list. With it, the ontology becomes a generative structure.

predicate family

3. Axiom System
3.1 Purpose of the Axiom Layer
The relational grammar states what kinds of relations are admissible. The axiom layer
states what structural claims are taken as irreducible starting commitments of the mother-
body. An axiom here is not a slogan. It is a formal commitment with consequence for
representation, analysis, and reconstruction.
Axiom 3.1(Persistence Requires Maintenance). For every systemS that persists over a
nonzero horizon,
Persist(S)⇒∃M : maintained_by(S,M ). (28)
This axiom declares that persistence is never primitive. Every persistent system is
maintained.
Axiom 3.2(Maintenance Requires Energetic Input). For every systemS that persists,
Persist(S)⇒∃E : depends_on(S,E ). (29)
This axiom rules out self-grounding persistence. Maintenance must be energetically fed.

toward a constrained cost-minimizing form:

This axiom does not claim global optimality in an idealized sense. It claims structural
selection pressure toward comparatively cheaper stabilization under actual constraints.

3.2 Axiom Interaction
These axioms are not independent ornaments. They form a generative cluster. Axioms 1
and 2 define the maintenance basis of persistence. Axiom 3 defines the selection pressure
on control. Axiom 4 defines the tendency of cost displacement. Axiom 5 defines the
temporal accumulation of hidden weakness. Axiom 6 explains why systems can reproduce
their deep pattern even while changing their surface story. Axiom 7 shows how legitimacy
enters as a control-cost modifier. Axiom 8 specifies thresholded breakdown.
Together, they already imply a basic EP causal cycle:
Persistence⇒Maintenance⇒Energetic Dependence⇒Control Selection⇒Externalization and Suppression⇒Fragility Accumulation⇒Fracture or Reproduction.

4. Dynamics
4.1 Why the Dynamics Layer Matters
Sections 0–3 define the mother-body’s identity, object set, relational grammar, and
axiomatic commitments. They do not yet specify how an EP system actually evolves. The
dynamics module supplies that missing operational layer. It is the first point at which
EP-FCS v0 becomes more than a definitional specification.
The purpose of this section is therefore explicit: to define a state-transition methodology
and a first executable algorithmic skeleton. The result is still not a fully calibrated
computational architecture, but it is already a genuine operating method.
